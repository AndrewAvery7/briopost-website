How to create a carousel post on LinkedIn, step by step

A LinkedIn carousel is a document post: you upload a PDF and people swipe through its pages. Here are the seven steps to one that people finish, with or without a design tool.

How to plan the slides, step by step

  1. 1

    Write your idea

    Type it in “Your idea (slide 1)”. It becomes the cover, so say what people get for swiping.

  2. 2

    Add your points

    Type one point per line. Each line becomes a slide, and bullets or numbers at the start of a line are left off.

  3. 3

    Check the plan

    Pick 4:5, 1:1 or 9:16 to see the outlines. A slide with an orange border may be hard to read on a phone: split it into two lines.

  4. 4

    Add the next step and copy

    Say what readers should do after the last slide, then choose “Copy as plain text” and take the plan into your design tool or Briopost.

The seven steps

  1. Plan one idea
  2. Write a hook for the cover
  3. Put one point on each slide
  4. Design for a phone
  5. Export it as a PDF
  6. Post it as a document
  7. Write the caption

  1. 01

    Plan one idea

    Write the idea as one sentence before you open any tool. “Three questions that turn a sales call into a conversation” is one idea. “Everything I know about sales” is twenty. If your sentence needs an “and”, you probably have two carousels.

    Under that sentence, list the points that support it, one per line. That list becomes your slides.

  2. 02

    Write a hook for the cover

    The cover is the only slide most people see in the feed, so it has to earn the swipe. Say what the reader gets, or name a problem they recognize, in a short headline. Add one line under it only if the headline needs help.

    Specific covers work better than clever ones. A number, a clear promise or an opinion someone could disagree with all give people a reason to swipe.

    Covers made with Briopost designs. The words are sample text.

  3. 03

    Put one point on each slide

    Give each slide one point: a heading and a sentence or two. If a slide needs a paragraph, split it. Use the middle slides for things words do badly on their own, like a chart from your numbers, a before and after, or one big number.

    Many guides suggest 5 to 15 slides. The right count is your list of points, plus a cover and a last slide. Make the last slide ask for one thing: an answer in the comments, a save, or a visit to a page.

  4. 04

    Design for a phone

    Most people will read your carousel on a phone, so design at full size and then check it small. A 4:5 portrait slide, 1080 by 1350 pixels, fills more of a phone screen than a square. More on sizes.

    Keep the text large and the lines short. Leave space at the edges, keep strong contrast between the words and the background, and use the same fonts, colors and layout on every slide so the carousel reads as one piece.

  5. 05

    Export it as a PDF

    LinkedIn builds the carousel from a document, and a PDF is the usual choice. Export from your design tool at the full slide size. A PDF made from screenshots, or from slides smaller than they appear, is the usual reason a carousel looks blurry.

    Check that every page is the same size and that your fonts came through. Then open the PDF on your phone once before you post it.

  6. 06

    Post it as a document

    On LinkedIn, start a post, choose the option to add a document, pick your PDF, and give the document a title when LinkedIn asks for one. Make the title clear, since it describes the document.

    LinkedIn changes its menus from time to time. If a button has moved, LinkedIn Help’s page on uploading and sharing documents has the current steps. Our guide to posting a PDF carousel on LinkedIn goes through them, with LinkedIn’s own file limits.

  7. 07

    Write the caption

    The text above the carousel decides whether people open it. Start with your hook in plain words, say in a line or two what the slides cover, and end with a question people can answer in a comment.

    Keep it short, since the slides do the explaining. Then stay around for the first comments and reply to them.

Questions

Carousel questions

How many slides should a LinkedIn carousel have?

As many as the idea needs. Many guides suggest about 5 to 15. Start from your list of points: one slide for each, plus a cover and a last slide.

What should the first slide say?

A clear hook: what the reader gets, or a problem they recognize, in a short headline. It’s the only slide most people see before they decide to swipe.

What should the last slide say?

One next step, such as a question to answer in the comments or a reason to save the post.

Why is my PDF carousel blurry?

Usually because the slides were exported smaller than they are shown, or the PDF was made from screenshots. Export at the full slide size, such as 1080 by 1350 pixels.

Can I make a LinkedIn carousel without a designer?

Yes. Keep to one or two fonts and a few colors, and use the same layout on every slide. A carousel tool such as Briopost can apply your brand kit to every slide for you.

How do I plan the slides of a carousel?

Type your idea, then one point per line, in the slide planner on this page. It lays out a hook, one slide per point and a next step, and flags any slide with too many words to read on a phone.
