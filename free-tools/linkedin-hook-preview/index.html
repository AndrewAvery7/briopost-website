How to use it

Test a LinkedIn hook in four steps.

  1. 1

    Write your opening

    Type the first line or two of your post in Hook 1. Two sample hooks are filled in so you can see how it works: replace them with yours.

  2. 2

    Add other versions

    Choose “Add another hook” to try a second or third way to say it. You can compare up to three side by side.

  3. 3

    Check the cut

    Each preview shows roughly where “…see more” falls on a phone, and the line under each box says whether the whole hook shows before it.

  4. 4

    Copy the one that works

    Choose “Copy hook” under the version that makes its point before the cut, and paste it at the top of your post.

Good to know

What makes a first line worth the click.

Be specific

Name the moment, the person or the number. “I changed jobs” is news to no one. “I left a steady job to open a bakery, and the first month nearly ended it” gives a reader something to picture.

Use a number or a tension

A count tells readers what they will get (“four steps”, “three questions”). A tension makes them want the answer: a before and after, a mistake, or two things that don’t seem to fit together.

Say it in plain words

Write the line the way you would say it to a colleague over coffee. Short words read faster on a phone, and your opening has only a few lines to land.

Promise only what the post delivers

Skip the clickbait. If the opening oversells, readers notice by the third line and stop trusting the next post. A hook works best when the rest of the post pays it off.

Questions

Hook questions

Where does LinkedIn cut a post with “…see more”?

It varies. The feed usually shows the first few lines of a post, fewer on a phone, and line breaks use up lines quickly. LinkedIn decides the exact cut, so this preview is an approximation of a phone screen, not a rule.

How long should a LinkedIn hook be?

There is no set length. A useful test: your whole opening idea shows in the preview before “…see more”. For many posts that means one or two short sentences.

Should I put a line break after my first line?

It can make the first line stand on its own. Each break also uses one of the few lines the feed shows before the cut, so check the preview after you add one.

Is anything I type sent or saved?

No. The preview runs in your browser. Nothing you type leaves your device or is stored, so reloading the page clears your hooks.
