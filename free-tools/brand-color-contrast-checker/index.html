Brand color contrast checker

Some brand colors look great on a website and are hard to read on a slide. Pick a text color and a background color, see the contrast, and get the closest shade that passes when it doesn’t.

Runs in your browser. Nothing you pick is sent anywhere or stored.

Type a hex code like #1F2A44, or click the swatch to pick a color.

Big type, like a headline

Normal text, like the words on a slide or the note under a chart.

Uses the contrast formula from the web’s accessibility guidelines (WCAG). Big type means about 24 pixels, or 19 pixels in bold. Briopost’s readability guard uses the same levels.

How to use it

Check your brand colors in four steps.

  1. 1

    Pick your text color

    Click the swatch to choose it, or type its hex code from your brand kit or website, like #1F2A44.

  2. 2

    Pick the background

    Do the same for the color behind the text. “Swap colors” checks it the other way round, light text on dark.

  3. 3

    Read the result

    The preview shows both sizes on a slide. Normal text needs 4.5 to 1. Big type, like a slide headline, needs 3 to 1.

  4. 4

    Fix it and keep it

    If normal text fails, choose “Use this text color” for the closest shade that passes, and put that color in your brand kit.

Good to know

What makes text readable on a slide.

What contrast measures

Contrast compares how light the text is with how light the background is, from 1 to 1 (the same color) to 21 to 1 (black on white). Hue doesn’t count: two colors can look very different and still be too close in lightness to read.

Why 4.5 and 3

They are the minimums in the web’s accessibility guidelines (WCAG). Big type, about 24 pixels or 19 pixels in bold, is easier to read, so it needs less. Briopost’s readability guard uses the same levels on every slide.

Phones make it harder

Most people read LinkedIn on a phone, often outside or with the screen dimmed. Text that only just passes on your monitor can be hard to read there, so more contrast is safer.

Keep the brand, change the shade

You rarely need a new color. A darker or lighter shade of the same hue usually passes, and that is what the checker suggests.

Questions

Contrast questions

What contrast do I need for text on a slide?

At least 4.5 to 1 for normal text and 3 to 1 for big type, the levels in the web’s accessibility guidelines (WCAG). Briopost’s readability guard uses the same levels.

Do I have to change my brand color to pass?

Usually only its shade. The checker suggests the closest lighter or darker shade of your text color that passes, so it still looks like your brand.

What counts as big type?

About 24 pixels, or about 19 pixels in bold. Slide headlines usually count; captions and the small print under a chart do not.

Is anything I pick sent or saved?

No. The checker runs in your browser. The colors you pick never leave your device and are not stored.
