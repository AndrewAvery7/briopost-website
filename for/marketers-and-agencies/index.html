Five post ideas

Five posts you could write this month.

Each one works on its own, with or without Briopost. Use your own stories and numbers; the opening lines are only examples.

  1. The question the client’s customers ask

    Ask the client’s sales or support team for the question they hear most, then answer it in the client’s voice.

    A first line to start fromEvery week someone asks us whether this works for small teams. Short answer: yes.

    Format: Text post, or a carousel with one slide per part of the answer.

  2. Behind one number

    Take a figure from the client’s own report or case study and explain what caused it. Use only numbers the client approved for public use.

    A first line to start fromLast quarter, support tickets fell by a third. Here is what changed.

    Format: Carousel, started from the client’s PDF or web page, with one chart.

  3. Cut the long piece into slides

    A blog post, webinar or guide the client already published holds five slides’ worth of ideas. Keep one idea per slide and link the full piece in the first comment.

    A first line to start fromFive things from our pricing guide that most readers skip.

    Format: Carousel, started from a link to the article.

  4. What people believe, and what is true

    Pick a common belief in the client’s field and set it against what their experience shows.

    A first line to start fromMost people think onboarding is a one-week job. It rarely is.

    Format: Comparison slide, ticks and crosses, two columns.

  5. The person behind the work

    Introduce one team member and what they do for customers, with their permission and their own photo.

    A first line to start fromMeet the person who answers your questions at 8 a.m.

    Format: Graphic, a single photo post with their words on it.

What helps most

The parts of Briopost that matter for you.

Everything below works today for beta testers. AI actions show their cost in credits before you run them.

01Keep every client separate

  • Save a brand kit per client: colors, fonts and logo that every design follows.
  • Start a client’s brand kit from their website address.
  • Work in more than one workspace, for example one per client.

02Get sign-off without the email chain

  • Send a review link. Clients approve or comment without an account.
  • A link holds up to 20 posts and stays open for 14 days. If you edit a post after it was approved, Briopost asks again.
  • Everything waiting for your OK sits in one list.

03Plan and deliver

  • See every post on a calendar, by month, week, day or list.
  • Plan a run of posts in one go. They land as drafts.
  • Download as a PDF, as images or as a short video, or share a carousel with a link.

04Work as a team

  • Invite your team. Owners, admins and members each get the right controls.
  • Save any carousel as your own template.
  • You see what an AI action will cost before you run it.

Questions

Short answers.

How do I keep LinkedIn posts on brand across clients?

Give each client a brand kit, or a workspace of their own. Every design follows the kit’s colors, fonts and logo, so one client’s look never slips into another’s.

Do clients need an account to approve posts?

No. A review link lets them approve or comment without signing up. If you change a post after they approved it, Briopost asks them again.
