What a brand kit is, and why it helps

A brand kit is a short list of choices you make once: your colors, your fonts, your logo, your name and the way you write. Every post then uses the same choices.

That sameness is the point. When your carousels and graphics look and sound alike, people can start to recognize them as yours before they read your name.

It also saves time. You stop picking colors and fonts for every post and spend that time on what the post says.

What goes in a brand kit

Five parts. Keep each one short, so it is easy to use the same way every time.

  • Colors, each with a job

    A background, a text color and one accent for the thing that matters most on a slide. An accent used everywhere stops standing out.

  • Two fonts

    One for headings and one for everything else. Two is plenty for a slide people read on a phone.

  • Your logo

    Or your photo, if you are the brand. Use it in the same place on every post.

  • Name and handle

    Your name, your handle or website, and a page number. They can sit quietly on every slide.

  • Your voice

    How you write: who you help, what you offer, the next step you want readers to take, and the tone you use.

Three ways to start a kit in Briopost

Pick whichever is quickest for you. You can change anything afterward.

  • From your website

    Paste your website address. Briopost reads the page and drafts a kit with your colors, fonts, logo and writing voice for you to review.

  • From a picture

    Use a banner, a flyer or a screenshot you already like. Briopost measures the colors in it.

  • By hand

    Start from one of 10 ready color sets, choose from 40 fonts and add your logo yourself.

What the website import reads

  • Colors and fonts used on the page. Briopost picks the closest of its 40 fonts and tells you when it used a different one.
  • Your logo and photos, saved with the kit.
  • Your writing voice: your name, tagline, who you help, the next step you want, your offer, your writing style and any guidance for the writing.

Reading your voice uses AI, which uses credits. Briopost shows the cost before it runs.

You review it first

The import gives you a draft. You check the colors, fonts, logo and voice, change what isn’t right, and only then save the kit.

Text that stays readable

Some brand colors look great on a website and hard to read on a slide. Briopost’s readability guard keeps text at a contrast of at least 4.5 to 1 against what is behind it, or 3 to 1 for big type. Those are the levels the web’s accessibility guidelines use.

So you can use your own colors without checking every slide by eye. To check a pair of colors yourself, use the contrast checker below.

Big type, like a headline

Normal text, like the words on a slide or the note under a chart.

Uses the contrast formula from the web’s accessibility guidelines (WCAG). Big type means about 24 pixels, or 19 pixels in bold. Briopost’s readability guard uses the same levels.

How to check your colors, step by step

  1. 1

    Pick your text color

    Click the swatch to choose it, or type its hex code from your brand kit or website, like #1F2A44.

  2. 2

    Pick the background

    Do the same for the color behind the text. “Swap colors” checks it the other way round, light text on dark.

  3. 3

    Read the result

    The preview shows both sizes on a slide. Normal text needs 4.5 to 1. Big type, like a slide headline, needs 3 to 1.

  4. 4

    Fix it and keep it

    If normal text fails, choose “Use this text color” for the closest shade that passes, and put that color in your brand kit.
