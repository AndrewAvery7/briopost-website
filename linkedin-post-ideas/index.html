LinkedIn post ideas: what to post, by goal and role

If you’re staring at an empty box wondering what to post on LinkedIn, start here. 75 concrete ideas for job seekers, consultants, founders, marketers and any professional, with the format that suits each group.

How to pick what to post

Most people don’t run out of ideas. They run out of ideas that feel worth posting. A few questions help:

  • Who should read it? Name one kind of reader, such as a hiring manager or a first-time founder, and write for them.
  • What should they do afterward? Reply, remember you, hire you, or just understand something. Pick one.
  • What do you know from experience? A real story from your week beats a general tip.
  • Can you say it in one sentence? If not, split it into two posts.

Where ideas come from

Look at the questions people asked you this month, the work you finished, the notes you took in meetings and the things that annoyed you. Each is a post in rough form.

Keep a running list. Choose a few ideas and give each a day in your LinkedIn content planner.

Match the idea to a format

The same idea can land differently as a short post, a carousel, a poll or a graphic. Choose by what the idea is, not by what’s fashionable.

  • Text post

    Best for stories, opinions and short lessons. Put the point in the first two lines. See post formatting and the hook examples.

  • Carousel

    Best for steps, checklists, frameworks and breakdowns. Each slide holds one idea. See how to make a LinkedIn carousel.

  • Poll

    Best for a quick question to your audience that you’ll follow up on. See LinkedIn polls.

  • Graphic

    Best for one number, one quote or one diagram. See LinkedIn graphics.

75 LinkedIn post ideas, by goal and role

Find your group, scan the ideas, and make each one yours with a real detail. The best formats for each group are listed first.

    • A weekly update on what you’re learning while you search.

When you write about clients, customers or colleagues, leave out names and details they haven’t agreed to share.

Turn an idea into a post

  1. Write the one thing. Finish the sentence “The point is…” in plain words.
  2. Add one real detail. A moment, a number or the name of a tool makes the point believable.
  3. Write the opening last. Try three versions and keep the clearest. The hook examples show nine patterns.
  4. Close with a next step. A question, a link or nothing at all. Skip the forced call to action.
  5. Plan it. A post on the calendar gets written. A post in your head usually doesn’t.

Questions

Post idea questions

What should I post on LinkedIn?

Things you know from your own work: a lesson from this week, a project and what you learned, a question your clients ask, a mistake and the fix. Pick one reader and one point per post.

How often should I post on LinkedIn?

As often as you can keep up without the quality dropping. A steady rhythm you can hold, such as once or twice a week, beats a burst followed by silence. Plan a few posts at a time on a content calendar.

What if I don’t have anything interesting to say?

You probably do. Look at the questions people asked you this month and the work you finished. Specific detail from a real job is what other people in your field want to read.

Are carousels better than text posts?

Neither is better in general. Use a carousel when the idea is steps, a checklist or a framework, and a text post when it’s a story or an opinion. See how to make a LinkedIn carousel.

Should job seekers post on LinkedIn?

It can help people see how you think. Share what you’re learning, a project you’re proud of and what you’re looking for. Keep it honest and useful, and see the page for job seekers.

Can I post about my clients?

Only with their permission, and without names or private details. Many consultants describe the problem and the approach and leave out who it was for.

How do I keep track of ideas?

Keep one running list and add to it whenever something happens at work. When you plan the month, pick the ideas that fit your goal and put them on the calendar.
