How to post a PDF carousel on LinkedIn

A LinkedIn carousel is a PDF shared as a document. Export your slides as one PDF, upload it, give it a title and write the text above it. Here are the five steps, and fixes for the usual problems.

The five steps

  1. Export your slides as one PDF
  2. Check the PDF before you post
  3. Start a post and add a document
  4. Give the document a title
  5. Write the text above it and post

  1. 01

    Export your slides as one PDF

    Put every slide in one file, in the order you want people to swipe. Each page of the PDF becomes one slide of the carousel.

    Export straight from your design tool at full size, such as 1080 by 1350 pixels. Don’t build the PDF from screenshots, since that is the most common cause of blurry slides.

  2. 02

    Check the PDF before you post

    Open the PDF on your phone once. Every page should be the same size and shape, the slides should be in order, and your fonts should look the way they did in your design.

    Also check that nothing private is inside, such as notes, client names or an email address you didn’t mean to share. People who can see the post can download the document.

  3. 03

    Start a post and add a document

    On LinkedIn on a computer, click Start a post, then More, then the Add a document icon. Click Choose file and pick your PDF. LinkedIn also lets you pick a file from a cloud drive it connects to.

    You can share a document from a Group or a LinkedIn Page too. LinkedIn moves its buttons from time to time, so if one has moved, check LinkedIn Help’s page on uploading and sharing documents.

  4. 04

    Give the document a title

    LinkedIn asks for a title, and the title shows on the document in the post. Make it say what the reader gets, in a few plain words, such as “Three questions for your next sales call”.

    Avoid a file name like “carousel-final-v3”. Then click Done.

  5. 05

    Write the text above it and post

    Add a description: the text people read above the document. Start with your hook in plain words, say in a line or two what the slides cover, and end with a question people can answer in a comment. Hashtags and @mentions are optional.

    Read it once more, then click Post.

LinkedIn’s own limits

LinkedIn sets the rules for document uploads, and it can change them. Here is what its Help pages said when we checked on November 17, 2026.

  • On file size and length, LinkedIn Help says: “The file size cannot exceed 100MB and 300 pages.”
  • It lists PPT, PPTX, DOC, DOCX and PDF as the file types you can upload.
  • On post text, LinkedIn Help says: “The character limit for a post is 3000 characters.”

Sources: Upload and share documents on LinkedIn and LinkedIn Help on post length, both checked November 17, 2026.

After you post

You can’t swap the document. LinkedIn Help says you can edit the description or remove the post, but you can’t change the document in it. If you spot a typo on a slide, the fix is to remove the post and share a corrected one. That is why step 2 matters.

People can download it. LinkedIn Help says members who can view the post can download the document as a PDF. Treat the file as public, and keep anything private out of it.

Source: Upload and share documents on LinkedIn, checked November 17, 2026.

When something looks wrong

Each of these is fixed in the PDF itself. Fix the file, then post it again.

ProblemLikely causeFix
Blurry slidesSlides exported smaller than they show, or a PDF made from screenshotsExport at full size, 1080 pixels wide, straight from your design tool.
Wrong size or shapePages of different sizes, or a page size your tool picked for youSet one slide size before you design, such as 1080 by 1350, and keep every page the same.
Fonts missing or swappedThe fonts weren’t included in the PDFTurn on the option to include fonts when you export, or pick fonts your tool can include.
Slides out of orderPages were exported or merged in the wrong orderReorder the slides in your design tool and export again as one file.
File too bigVery large photos inside the slidesUse smaller copies of your photos, or fewer of them, and check LinkedIn’s own limits above.

Questions

PDF carousel questions

How do I post a PDF carousel on LinkedIn?

Start a post, choose to add a document, pick your PDF, give it a title and click Done. Then write the text above it and click Post.

Can I change the PDF after I post it?

No. LinkedIn Help says you can edit the description or remove the post, but you can’t change the document. Check the PDF on your phone before you post.

Can people download my carousel?

Yes. LinkedIn Help says members who can view the post can download the document as a PDF, so keep anything private out of it.

Can I upload a PowerPoint file instead of a PDF?

LinkedIn Help lists PPT and PPTX among the file types you can upload. A PDF is the safer choice, since it keeps your layout as you exported it.

Why is my PDF carousel blurry?

Usually because the slides were exported smaller than they show, or the PDF was made from screenshots. Export at full size, such as 1080 by 1350 pixels.

How do I check my PDF before I upload it?

Choose it in the free size checker on this page. It shows the page size, shape and page count, and whether every page matches. Nothing is uploaded.
