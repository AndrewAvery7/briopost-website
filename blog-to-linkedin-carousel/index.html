Guide

Turn a blog post or article into a LinkedIn carousel

A good article already has the idea and the proof. Your job is to cut it down to one point per slide, keep the quotes accurate, and give credit. Here is how, and how Briopost helps.

From article to carousel in seven steps

  1. Find the one idea
  2. Cut it to the points that carry it
  3. One point per slide, usually 6 to 10 slides
  4. Keep quotes and numbers accurate
  5. Credit it, and ask if it isn’t yours
  6. Write a new hook
  7. End with one next step

  1. 01

    Find the one idea

    Read the article once without taking notes. Then write down, in one sentence, what a reader should remember. Often it’s the promise in the headline or the strongest section, not the whole piece.

    A long article can hold three or four carousels. Choose one and keep the rest for later posts.

  2. 02

    Cut it to the points that carry it

    Go back through the article and pull out the points that support your sentence: the steps, the examples, the one number that proves it. Skip the introduction, the background and anything that repeats.

    Put each point in plain words. People read a slide in a few seconds, so a point that needs a paragraph probably needs splitting.

  3. 03

    One point per slide, usually 6 to 10 slides

    Give each point its own slide with a short heading and a sentence or two. A cover, four to eight points and a last slide is a common shape.

    The idea sets the count, not a rule. A list of three mistakes is short; a ten-step method needs more room. If you’re stretching thin points to fill slides, cut them.

  4. 04

    Keep quotes and numbers accurate

    Quote people word for word, and name who said it. Copy numbers as the article gives them, with the unit and the date. If the article cites a study, name the study, not just the article.

  5. 05

    Credit it, and ask if it isn’t yours

    If it’s your own post, say so and link the full article in the caption. If someone else wrote it, ask before you turn their work into slides, credit the author and the publication by name, and link the original.

    A summary in your own words with credit is a different thing from copying their text or pictures onto your slides. When in doubt, ask.

  6. 06

    Write a new hook

    Blog titles are often written for search, and they read long in a feed. Write the cover for the person scrolling: what they get, or a problem they recognize, in a few words. For example, “How to Write a Project Update Email: A Complete Guide” could become “Your project updates are too long.”

  7. 07

    End with one next step

    Make the last slide ask for one thing: read the full article, answer a question, or save the carousel. If you want people to read the article, put the link in the caption.

With Briopost

How Briopost turns an article into a carousel

Give Briopost the address of the article. It reads the page and drafts the slides, and you edit every one.

  1. Paste the link

    Paste the address of a public web page: a blog post, an article or a newsletter issue. Briopost reads the page. If it read the same page in the last 24 hours, it uses that copy.

  2. Or send it from your browser

    The “Send to Briopost” bookmark sends the page you’re on: its address, its title and up to 2,000 characters you selected. Nothing is read until you click “Read this page”. It’s a bookmark, not a browser extension.

  3. The assistant drafts the slides

    It writes a first draft from what it read, in your brand kit’s colors, fonts and logo. You see about what that costs before it runs.

  4. You edit, then export or plan

    Click any word to change it, check the quotes and add the credit. Export a PDF, images or a short video, or put the carousel on your calendar, where it waits for your OK.

When a page can’t be read

  • Pages behind a sign-in. If the article needs a login or a subscription, Briopost can’t read it. It tells you, and you can paste the text instead.
  • Sites that block automated readers. Some sites turn away tools like Briopost. The answer is the same: paste the text.
  • Pasting doesn’t change who owns it. If the article is someone else’s, ask before you share a carousel made from it.
  • The draft is a start. Check every slide against the article before it goes anywhere.

Questions

Article to carousel questions

How do I turn a blog post into a LinkedIn carousel?

Pick the one idea, pull out the points that support it, and give each point its own slide. Write a new hook for the cover, end with one next step, and credit the article.

Can I turn someone else’s article into a carousel?

Ask the author or publisher first. If they agree, credit them by name on the slides and link the original in the caption.

What if Briopost can’t read the page?

Some pages need a sign-in or block automated readers. Briopost tells you when that happens, and you can paste the article’s text instead.

Does it work with newsletters?

Yes, if the issue has a public web page. Paste its address. If it only arrived by email, paste the text.

Will Briopost post the carousel for me?

Not on its own. You edit every slide, and nothing goes to LinkedIn until you confirm it yourself.
