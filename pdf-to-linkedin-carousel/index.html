Turn a PDF into a LinkedIn carousel

A long PDF can hold a dozen carousels. Pick the one idea your readers need, give each point its own slide, and say where it came from. Here is how to do it by hand, and how Briopost helps.

From PDF to carousel in seven steps

  1. Find the one idea
  2. Keep only what supports it
  3. Give each point its own slide
  4. Copy numbers and quotes exactly
  5. Credit the source, and check you may use it
  6. Write a new hook
  7. End with one next step

  1. 01

    Find the one idea

    Read the summary and the conclusions first. A report, a white paper or a slide deck usually holds several findings, and a carousel carries one. Pick the finding your readers would most want to know, and write it as a single sentence.

    If you can’t choose, ask which finding would change what your reader does next week. That one goes first. The others can be later carousels.

  2. 02

    Keep only what supports it

    Go through the pages with your sentence beside you. Mark the numbers, examples and short quotes that back it up. Leave out the method, the background and the side findings; people who want them can read the full document.

    A simple test: if a point doesn’t make your sentence clearer or easier to believe, it stays in the PDF.

  3. 03

    Give each point its own slide

    One point per slide: a short heading and a sentence or two. If a slide needs a paragraph, split it, or move the detail to the caption.

    Most carousels made from a document land between 6 and 10 slides: a cover, four to eight points and a last slide. The idea sets the count, not a rule. Three findings make a short carousel; a seven-step process needs more room. If your list runs well past ten, you probably have two carousels.

  4. 04

    Copy numbers and quotes exactly

    Take each figure as the document gives it, with its unit and its year. Round only when the rounded number still means the same thing. Quote people word for word, and name who said it.

    If you redraw a chart, use the document’s own numbers and say where they came from. A slide is easy to screenshot and share, so a wrong number can travel further than the original.

  5. 05

    Credit the source, and check you may use it

    Name the document and who published it, on the slide that uses it and again on the last slide or in the caption. If it’s your company’s report, check with whoever owns it before you share it.

    If someone else wrote it, ask the author or publisher before you reuse their charts, pictures or long passages. A short quote with credit is a different thing from copying their pages.

  6. 06

    Write a new hook

    A report title is written for a shelf, not for a feed. Write the cover from the finding instead, in the words your reader uses. For example, “Annual Customer Survey” says what the document is; “What customers told us about onboarding” says what the reader gets.

  7. 07

    End with one next step

    Make the last slide ask for one thing: read the full report, answer a question in the comments, or save the carousel for later. One ask is easier to act on than three. If you point to the full report, put its address in the caption.

How to plan the slides, step by step

  1. 1

    Write your idea

    Type it in “Your idea (slide 1)”. It becomes the cover, so say what people get for swiping.

  2. 2

    Add your points

    Type one point per line. Each line becomes a slide, and bullets or numbers at the start of a line are left off.

  3. 3

    Check the plan

    Pick 4:5, 1:1 or 9:16 to see the outlines. A slide with an orange border may be hard to read on a phone: split it into two lines.

  4. 4

    Add the next step and copy

    Say what readers should do after the last slide, then choose “Copy as plain text” and take the plan into your design tool or Briopost.

Two different jobs

Making slides from a PDF is not the same as posting one

This page is about the start: you have a long PDF, such as a report, and you want a short carousel from it. The carousel you finish is also a PDF, and posting it on LinkedIn is the last step. Our guide on how to post a PDF carousel on LinkedIn covers that part, from exporting the slides to writing the text above them.

One limit matters for both. LinkedIn Help says of uploaded documents: “The file size cannot exceed 100MB and 300 pages.” (Upload and share documents on LinkedIn, checked November 17, 2026). You can’t change the document once it’s posted, so open it on your phone before you share it.

With Briopost

How Briopost turns a PDF into a carousel

Briopost reads the PDF and drafts the slides. You still choose what stays, and you check every slide.

  1. Upload the PDF

    Briopost takes a PDF of up to 50 MB and 100 pages. Before it reads anything, it shows the page count and about what reading it will cost, so you decide whether to go on.

  2. It reads in the background

    Reading shows its progress and tries again by itself if a step doesn’t go through. Briopost reads the text, and it can keep up to about 20 pictures from the PDF for your slides.

  3. The assistant drafts the slides

    From what it read, the assistant writes a first draft in your brand kit’s colors, fonts and logo. You see about what that costs before it runs.

  4. You check every slide

    Click any word to change it. Check each number and quote against the PDF, add the credit, and cut what you don’t need. Then export a PDF, images or a short video, or plan the post. Nothing posts without your OK.

What to know before you start

  • Password-protected PDFs. Save a copy without the password first, then upload that copy.
  • Scanned pages. Briopost reads the text in a PDF. If your pages are pictures of paper, it may find little or no text, so we don’t promise those work. Copy the words you need and start from notes instead.
  • Big files. Over 50 MB or 100 pages, save just the pages you need as a new PDF and upload that.
  • The draft is a start. The assistant can misread a table or miss the point of a page. Read every slide before it leaves Briopost.

Questions

PDF to carousel questions

Can I turn a PDF into a LinkedIn carousel?

Yes. Pick the one idea in the PDF worth sharing, give each point its own slide, and export the slides as a new PDF to post. Briopost can read the PDF and draft the slides for you to edit.

How many slides should a carousel from a report have?

Often 6 to 10: a cover, a few points and a last slide. Let the idea set the count. If the list runs well past ten, split it into two carousels.

What PDFs can Briopost read?

PDFs of up to 50 MB and 100 pages that contain text. Save a password-protected PDF without its password first. We don’t promise that scanned pages work; paste the text instead.

Does reading a PDF cost anything?

Reading uses AI, which costs money. Briopost shows the page count and about what it will cost before it starts. During the beta, that comes out of your included AI credits.

Is this the same as posting a PDF carousel?

No. This page is about making a carousel from a long PDF. Posting the finished carousel on LinkedIn is the last step, and our guide on how to post a PDF carousel on LinkedIn covers it.

How do I plan slides from a long PDF?

Pick the one idea, then type it and the points that support it, one per line, into the free slide planner on this page. It shows the slide-by-slide plan and flags crowded slides.
