What makes a carousel worth swiping

People decide on the first slide whether to swipe, and they leave as soon as a slide stops paying off. The carousels people finish tend to share a few habits.

  • One idea. If you can’t say it in one sentence, you have two carousels.
  • A cover that makes a promise. The slides after it keep that promise.
  • One point per slide, short enough to read in a few seconds.
  • Type you can read on a phone, with strong contrast and room at the edges.
  • A last slide that asks for one thing, such as a comment or a save.

The step-by-step guide covers each of these, from planning the idea to posting the PDF.

How to plan the slides, step by step

  1. 1

    Write your idea

    Type it in “Your idea (slide 1)”. It becomes the cover, so say what people get for swiping.

  2. 2

    Add your points

    Type one point per line. Each line becomes a slide, and bullets or numbers at the start of a line are left off.

  3. 3

    Check the plan

    Pick 4:5, 1:1 or 9:16 to see the outlines. A slide with an orange border may be hard to read on a phone: split it into two lines.

  4. 4

    Add the next step and copy

    Say what readers should do after the last slide, then choose “Copy as plain text” and take the plan into your design tool or Briopost.

How it works

How Briopost makes a carousel

  1. Start from what you have

    Type a few words or paste rough notes. Paste a link and Briopost turns the article into slides. Upload a PDF and start from what it says, or drop in your own photos and they fill the picture slots. No photos? Briopost can fill the empty picture slots with stock photos.

  2. Get a first draft

    The assistant writes the slides and lays them out in one of 150 carousel designs. You see what that costs before it runs.

  3. Make it yours

    Your brand kit sets the colors, fonts and logo, and you can start the kit from your website address. Click any word on a slide and type. Add a chart from your own numbers, a comparison with ticks and crosses, or one big number.

  4. Export it or plan it

    Download a PDF, a set of images or a short video. Or put the carousel on your calendar, where it waits for your OK.

Start from 150 designs

Questions

Carousel maker questions

What is a LinkedIn carousel?

A post people swipe through, one slide at a time. LinkedIn builds it from a document you upload, usually a PDF. Briopost designs the slides and exports the PDF.

How do I make a LinkedIn carousel with AI?

Give Briopost a few words, your notes, a link or a PDF. It drafts the slides in your brand, and you edit any slide before you export a PDF or plan the post. Each AI step shows its cost before it runs.

Can I edit the slides myself?

Yes. Click any word on a slide and type. You can also change the colors, fonts and pictures, and add a slide from 21 ready layouts.

Will Briopost post the carousel for me?

No. Every post waits for your OK. See what we never do.

Do I have to connect my LinkedIn account?

No. Connecting LinkedIn is optional. You can download a carousel as a PDF or as images and post it yourself. Connect LinkedIn only if you want Briopost to post at the time you picked, after your OK.

Do I need my own AI account?

No. During the beta, AI credits are included, and you see the cost in credits before each AI action. If you prefer, you can use your own AI key.

Can I plan a carousel without signing up?

Yes. The free slide planner on this page runs in your browser with no account. Type your idea and points, then copy the plan into Briopost or your design tool.
