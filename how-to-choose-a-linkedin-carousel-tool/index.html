How to choose a LinkedIn carousel tool: 10 questions to ask

Most carousel tools can make a good-looking slide. The differences show up later: when something posts, what the AI costs, and where your words go. Ask these ten questions of any tool you try, ours included.

By the Briopost team · Last updated November 17, 2026

Each question says why it matters and what to look for. Under it, we say plainly what Briopost does today, and where the answer is “not yet”, we say that too. Briopost is in a free beta, by invitation.

The ten questions

  1. Does anything post without your OK? Briopost: never
  2. Does it show AI costs before you spend? Briopost: yes
  3. Can it use your brand kit? Briopost: yes
  4. A real editor, or fixed templates? Briopost: real editor
  5. Which export formats? Briopost: PDF, images, video
  6. Can clients approve before it goes out? Briopost: yes
  7. What does it send, and where? Briopost: answered below
  8. Can you take your data with you? Briopost: yes
  9. Does it work for a team? Briopost: yes
  10. Where can it post, and what waits on LinkedIn? Briopost: partly

1Does anything post without your OK?

A LinkedIn post goes out under your name. If a tool can post by itself, it can post the wrong draft, at the wrong time, or a draft you never read.

What to look for

  • Every post needs a deliberate confirm from you. A setting that is “on” by default doesn’t count.
  • If you change a post after approving it, the tool asks you again.
  • One place lists everything that is waiting for you.
  • AI helpers and connected apps can draft. They can’t confirm for you.

NeverBriopost today

Nothing posts without your OK. Every post waits for your confirm click, and everything waiting for you sits in one list. Change a post after confirming it and you confirm again. A connected assistant can draft and schedule, but it can’t confirm. What we never do

2Does it show AI costs before you spend?

AI writing and AI pictures cost money, and the price depends on how much you ask for. If you only see the cost later, on a bill, you can’t decide whether a rewrite is worth it.

What to look for

  • An estimate on the button, before anything runs.
  • A record of what each action cost.
  • A clear monthly allowance or limit.
  • The option to use your own AI key.

YesBriopost today

You see what an AI action will cost before you run it, and you choose Standard or Best each time. A usage page shows what each action cost. The beta is free, with a monthly AI allowance, and you can bring your own AI key if you prefer.

3Can it use your brand kit?

People recognize your posts by color and type before they read a word. Picking colors again for every carousel is slow, and small differences add up.

What to look for

  • Save your colors, fonts and logo once, and every design uses them.
  • Text stays readable when the colors change.
  • A quick way to start, such as reading your website.

YesBriopost today

Save your colors, fonts and logo once, and every carousel and graphic follows them. Start the kit from your website address, or from 10 ready color sets and 40 fonts. Your words stay readable on every color. See one design in three brand kits

4A real editor, or fixed templates?

A template gets you started. The draft still needs your words, your numbers and your judgment. If all you can do is swap text in fixed boxes, the idea ends up bending to fit the design.

What to look for

  • You can type straight onto the slide.
  • You can add, remove and reorder slides.
  • Charts from your own numbers, icons, and photos you can frame.
  • You can save your own designs as templates.

Real editorBriopost today

Click any word on a slide and type. Add a slide from 21 ready layouts. Add bar, line, area, pie, radar and gauge charts from your own numbers, pick from about 200 icons, and drag to frame your photos. Save any carousel as your own template, or start from 150 carousel designs. Browse the templates

5Which export formats?

LinkedIn builds a carousel from a document, usually a PDF. You may also want single images for other places, or a short video.

What to look for

  • A PDF that is ready to upload.
  • Each slide as an image.
  • The sizes you post in: portrait (1080 × 1350), square (1080 × 1080) or tall (1080 × 1920).
  • Downloads that look exactly like what you saw in the editor.

PDF, images, videoBriopost today

Download as a PDF, as images or as a short video. Carousels come in square, portrait or story size; graphics also come in wide and banner sizes. LinkedIn carousel sizes

6Can clients approve before it goes out?

If you post for a client or a manager, someone else has to see it first. Screenshots in email get lost, and asking reviewers to make an account slows everyone down.

What to look for

  • A review link you can send.
  • Reviewers can approve or comment without an account.
  • Editing a post after approval asks for approval again.
  • Links that expire.

YesBriopost today

Send a review link with up to 20 posts. Clients approve or comment without an account. The link lasts 14 days, and editing a post after approval asks again. Even after a client approves, nothing posts until you confirm it. Post approval

7What does it send, and where?

You paste drafts, client notes and numbers into these tools. Ask where each of them goes.

What to look for

  • What goes to AI services, and when.
  • What a public share link shows, and what it leaves out.
  • How it connects to LinkedIn: LinkedIn’s own sign-in, never your password.
  • Whether saved keys and connections are encrypted.
  • A privacy policy you can read in one sitting.

Answered hereBriopost today

  • Briopost connects through LinkedIn’s own sign-in and never sees your LinkedIn password. It’s a website, not a browser extension, and it doesn’t scrape LinkedIn.
  • An AI action sends what it needs to the AI service that does the work. That happens when you run one, or when you turn on a feature that runs on its own, like weekly post ideas.
  • A share link shows your slides and nothing else. The text you pasted, your slide notes and your caption stay private.
  • Voice notes are turned into text, and the recording isn’t kept.
  • If you upload LinkedIn’s analytics file, your browser reads it and sends only the numbers.
  • Your LinkedIn connection and any AI key you save are stored encrypted. We don’t sell your information.

Privacy Policy

8Can you take your data with you?

You’ll make a lot of work in a tool like this. If you ever leave, you should be able to keep it.

What to look for

  • A way to download everything you made.
  • A way to delete your account yourself.
  • Downloads in ordinary files that open anywhere.

YesBriopost today

The workspace owner can download everything the workspace made, and you can delete your account any time. A deleted account is held for 30 days in case you change your mind, then it’s gone for good. Downloads are ordinary PDF and image files.

9Does it work for a team?

Agencies, ghostwriters and small teams share drafts, calendars and brand kits. Not everyone should be able to connect accounts or change settings.

What to look for

  • Roles, so each person gets the right controls.
  • Separate spaces for separate clients.
  • One calendar everyone can see.

YesBriopost today

Invite your team. Owners, admins and members each get the right controls. Work in more than one workspace, for example one per client, and see every post on a calendar.

10Where can it post, and what waits on LinkedIn?

LinkedIn decides which tools may use some of its features, such as posting to Company Pages and reading post statistics. Ask which of these a tool can actually use today.

What to look for

  • Posting to your own profile.
  • Posting to a Company Page, if you need it.
  • Where its statistics come from.

PartlyBriopost today

You can connect your own LinkedIn profile and schedule posts there, each with your OK. Posting to Company Pages is not available yet: it waits on LinkedIn’s approval. Live post statistics from LinkedIn are not available yet either. Until then, you can upload LinkedIn’s own analytics file and see your numbers in Briopost, with a simple monthly report card.
