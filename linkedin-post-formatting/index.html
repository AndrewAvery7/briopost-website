LinkedIn post formatting that is easy to read

LinkedIn posts have no bold button and only a few lines show before “see more”. Here is how to bold a few words, break up the text and add lists, and when to hold back.

How bold and italic work in LinkedIn posts

The LinkedIn post box has no bold or italic button. The bold text you see in posts is made of special Unicode letters that look bold. A formatter swaps your normal letters for them, and you paste the result into LinkedIn.

For example, 𝗕𝗼𝗹𝗱 and 𝘪𝘵𝘢𝘭𝘪𝘤 here are not styled text. They are different characters.

That has side effects, so use them sparingly:

  • Screen readers may read them oddly. Some read each letter out on its own, and some skip the word.
  • Search may not match them. A bold word may not be found by someone searching for the normal word.
  • Some devices may show them differently. Older phones or apps can show plain boxes instead of letters.

A good rule: bold a few words that carry the point, such as the first line or a key phrase. Never bold a whole paragraph, your name, or words people search for.

Line breaks and white space

On a phone, a long paragraph turns into a solid block of text. Short paragraphs with space between them are easier to follow.

  • One idea per paragraph. When the idea changes, start a new one.
  • One to three lines each. Count lines on a phone, not on your laptop.
  • A blank line between ideas. It gives the eye a place to rest.
  • Mix the lengths. A one-line paragraph after a longer one stands out.

Write the post somewhere you can see the line breaks, such as the free formatter or the Briopost editor, then check the preview before you post.

Bullets and numbered lists

LinkedIn posts have no list button either. Lists are made from ordinary characters at the start of each line.

  • Pick one marker and keep it. A dot (•), an arrow (→) or a dash (–) all work. Mixing them looks untidy.
  • Number the list when order matters. “1.”, “2.”, “3.” are clearer than emoji numbers.
  • Keep each item to one line. If an item needs two sentences, it may need its own paragraph.
  • Put a line above the list that says what it is, such as “Three things I check before a call:”.

Screen readers may read some markers aloud, such as “right arrow”. A plain dot or a number is the quietest choice.

The first lines and the “see more” cut

LinkedIn shows the first few lines of a post, then hides the rest behind “…see more”. Those first lines have to make someone want to read on.

  • Lead with the point. Skip the warm-up. Start with the claim, the result or the question.
  • Make the first line short. One sentence you would say out loud.
  • Don’t spend the opening on line breaks. Empty lines at the top push your words below the cut.
  • Check where the cut falls. The free formatter and the Briopost editor both show it while you type.

The LinkedIn post generator page has more on writing a first line in your own voice.

Emoji and hashtags

Emoji: a few can add tone or mark a list. Many in a row are hard to read, and screen readers say the name of each one out loud. Use them where they add meaning, not as decoration.

Hashtags: if you use them, pick a few that fit the post and put them at the end. A long row of hashtags in the middle of a sentence is hard to read.

How long can a LinkedIn post be?

LinkedIn Help says: “The character limit for a post is 3000 characters.” (LinkedIn Help, checked November 17, 2026.)

That is a limit, not a target. Say what you need to say, then stop. If a post keeps growing, it may be two posts, or a carousel.

Questions

Formatting questions

How do I bold text in a LinkedIn post?

LinkedIn has no bold button for posts. Type your text in a formatter that swaps the letters for bold-looking Unicode letters, then paste it into LinkedIn. Briopost’s free post formatter does this in your browser.

Is bold text bad for accessibility?

It can be. Screen readers may read the special letters oddly or skip them, so keep bold to a few words and never put a whole post in it.

How do I add line breaks to a LinkedIn post?

Press Enter between paragraphs, and leave a blank line between ideas. Write the post in a formatter or the Briopost editor first so you can see how it will look.
